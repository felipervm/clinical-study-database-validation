03 — TRACEABILITY & UAT

From requirement to evidence.

A reviewer should be able to follow every edit check back to a specification and a positive or negative test. That traceability matters more than the volume of simulated data.

These are deterministic software demonstrations. They are not approved clinical UAT, release sign-off, or evidence of EDC vendor qualification.

Each rule in the list maps to a requirement and a machine-readable UAT scenario. View the full UAT matrix ↗

CDISC and FDA guidance are background reading for this exercise, not evidence of conformance. This sample is not CDISC-certified, a validated EDC system, or fit for regulated research use.

THE NEXT STEP / LEARN WITH THE TEAM

A foundation for the work.
Clear about the gaps.

This case demonstrates specification logic, traceable checks, test boundaries and read-only query review.

What a real EDC implementation would still require

Vendor-specific form and role configuration, study-approved specifications, access controls, audit trails, change control, integrations, query lifecycle, deployment qualification and formal UAT sign-off. None is implemented here.

04 — WHY I BUILT THIS

Quality is designed in.

I'm Felipe Mattos. My work spans data quality, programming, AI annotation QA and operational improvement. I built this sample after reading Everest's Clinical Database Designer I responsibilities, because I wanted to show how I'd approach the work—not only list tools on a résumé.

I haven't worked professionally with Medidata Rave, InForm or Clinical One. This project demonstrates transferable design and testing skills, and a practical way to learn the clinical data workflow with care.

I'd welcome a chance to contribute to a clinical data design or quality team and learn from experienced specialists.
